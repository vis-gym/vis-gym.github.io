In this experiment, you will make decisions based on weather forecasts. You will play the role of an electric grid operator who is monitoring an electrical circuit in an area where there is high fire danger. If you think the wind speed will go above 50 mph, your job is to turn off that electrical circuit to reduce the risk of fire. Each question will show you a graph of the past wind speed and a forecast of the future wind speed. The wind speed forecasts are uncertain, try to keep the circuit on when you can, and turn it off when you think there's too much risk of the wind speed going above 50 mph. On the next screen there will be an attention check question and the correct answer is graphs . You will not be paid for this experiment if you answer the question incorrectly.

In this experiment, you will not see the full set of forecasts. Instead, you will see the mean (average) of all of the forecasts and a 50% confidence interval. A 50% confidence interval is a range of values that has a 50% probability of containing the true future wind speed value. The example below shows the mean forecast as a dashed line and the 50% confidence interval as a blue band.

In the context of this experiment, you can assume that 50% of the wind speed forecasts fall inside of the 50% confidence interval. There is a 50% chance that the actual wind speed will fall in this range. There is a 25% chance that the actual wind speed will be higher than this range, and a 25% chance that it will be lower than this range. In the example shown above, the confidence interval reaches just below the 50 mph threshold. That means that there is a 25% chance that the wind speed will go above 50 mph and a 75% chance that it will stay below 50 mph.
