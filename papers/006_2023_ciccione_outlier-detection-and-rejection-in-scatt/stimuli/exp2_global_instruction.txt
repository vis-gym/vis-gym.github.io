Participants were invited to sit on a fixed chair with their head at a distance of 50 cm from the screen. Each experimental session was divided into five blocks of 80 trials; the duration of each block was 4 minutes. After each block, participants could take a short break. Before starting the actual experiment, 25 practice trials were run under the researcher's supervision, in order to control for the correct execution of the task (i.e., maintaining the correct distance from the screen, correctly placing their hand and fingers; familiarizing with the rapid presentation of the stimuli. No feedback on performance was provided). On each trial, as illustrated in Figure 1A, a fixation cross first appeared for 1,000 ms, immediately followed by a scatterplot flashed for 100 ms.

Participants were asked to perform the exact same task as in Experiment 1. The only difference consisted in the information given to them before starting the experimental session: participants were informed that one or more outliers, defined as points outside the main dataset, could be present in some trials. They were invited to try to exclude such outliers from their answers, and thus to perform both tasks of trend judgment and slope adjustment only on the main dataset.
